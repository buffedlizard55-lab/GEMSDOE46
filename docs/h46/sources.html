S1 · DrivenData competition 306 - GEMS Prize Challenge, problem description (metric, submission format, task definition)

https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/ · reached

  • DTI(alpha=0.2, beta=0.8) with triangular kernel k(d)=max(1-d/R,0), R=300 m
  • the three distance-weighted terms TPw / FPw / FNw as transcribed in src/gems46/metric.py
  • the worked example TPw=3.00, FPw=1.89, FNw=2.00 -> 0.60 (arithmetic reproduced in tests/test_metric.py)
  • submission format: single-band float32 GeoTIFF, EPSG:32611, 100 m, same bounds, values in [0,1], null/NaN outside
  • two-round structure: initial round scored on a private set of expert-labelled *new* faults; final round rescored on an expanded label set after expert review of submitted predictions

S4 · Peng, C.-K., Buldyrev, S. V., Havlin, S., Simons, M., Stanley, H. E., Goldberger, A. L. (1994). Mosaic organization of DNA nucleotides. Physical Review E 49(5), 1685-1689

https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.49.1685 · NOT reachable from the sandbox; checked via https://archive.physionet.org/physiotools/dfa/, https://cran.r-project.org/package=fractal

  • origin of detrended fluctuation analysis (DFA): integrate the series, split into boxes of length n, remove the local least-squares line in each box, take the RMS fluctuation F(n), and take the slope of log F(n) vs log n as the scaling exponent alpha
  • the estimator implemented in src/gems46/dfa.py follows this definition exactly (verified against an independent O(N) transcription in tests/test_dfa.py)

S5 · Varotsos, P. A., Sarlis, N. V., Skordas, E. S. (2009). Detrended fluctuation analysis of the magnetic and electric field variations that precede rupture. Chaos 19(2), 023114

https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3130931 · NOT reachable from the sandbox; checked via https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19566249/, https://arxiv.org/abs/0904.2465, https://pubs.aip.org/aip/cha/article/19/2/023114/909537/

  • pre-rupture magnetic field variations appear random at short time lags and become long-range correlated at lags above the mean inter-spike interval, with scaling exponent alpha around 0.9
  • pre-seismic electric field variations of several hours to days show scale invariance with alpha approximately 1 over about five orders of magnitude
  • IMPORTANT NUANCE (recorded so the hypothesis is not overstated): the paper's shift is a *crossover in time lag* within a time series - not a demonstration that a spatial scaling exponent changes across a geological fault. GEMSDOE46 adapts DFA to transects of a spatial grid; that adaptation is this project's hypothesis (H46-1), not a published result.

How decisions are made here — the two core values.
Maximize P(Win). Every choice is made against the official metric's own algebra (DTI = T/(0.2·S + 0.8·|G|), marginal rule k > 0.2·DTI), never against a proxy known to drift. Where the proxy and the algebra disagree — they do, see IR-46-04 — the algebra decides and the disagreement is filed.
Own the Outcome. End to end: the shipped bytes are re-read and audited, the estimator is verified against exact-spectral fGn and textbook DFA values, negative results are published as negative results (H46-1 measured weaker than the structural field), and the instrument that would falsify the next idea is shipped with it.
