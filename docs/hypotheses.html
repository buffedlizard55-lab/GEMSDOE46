H1 — release-controlled new-fault detection

  • Signature. Seismicity is a release field: its influence should decay like exp(−d/λ) from release points, so the feature is a unit-free proximity score (global rank of band 10, and exp(−d/median d)) multiplied by the structural layers. A tree cannot build that product by itself — and a monotone transform of band 10 alone would add nothing, because tree splits are invariant to monotone transforms of a single column. That is why the hypothesis is the interaction.
  • Why a missing fault. A fault already in the compilation is already drawn. What a surface compilation structurally cannot contain is a structure that has ruptured without a mapped scarp: the 2020 Mw 6.5 Monte Cristo Range earthquake produced a 28 km surface-rupture zone on largely unmapped parts of the Candelaria fault, incoherent with QFaults (USGS publication 70220306; Koehler et al. 2021 SRL). Its epicentre (38.169 °N, 117.850 °W) maps to row/col 2836/1822, verified inside the scored footprint.
  • How it differs. Band 10 is not in the shipped 25-feature plan at all; band 16 enters only as a plain smoothed value.

Descriptive follow-up, not a validation. In the shipped file, 373 dots lie within 10 km of the Monte Cristo epicentre — 0.93 % of that box against a global footprint density of 0.32 % (~2.9× the average local density). With no truth vector for an unmapped fault this is not a score, and it is not treated as one.

The other four, in one line each

  • H2 curvature-restoration residual. Structure tensor (coherence + orientation) and a local-plane residual on bands 6/2/12/19 — orientation consistency and tilt-invariance, which no shipped feature computes (they are single-scale Hessian ridges only). Source: Lindeberg 1998 IJCV 30(2):117–154.
  • H3 lineament-network topology. Skeletonise H2's lineaments, then distance-to-endpoint, orientation agreement across gaps, step-over distance and junction density — non-local features that target the links and step-overs where BRIDGE places the highest permeability. Source: BRIDGE final report.
  • H4 conductive-clay / cover coincidence. Conductivity high and gravity-gradient margin and thick cover (band 15): the classic hidden-fault signature, absent from the shipped plan because band 17 is used only as a plain smoothed column. Sources: BRIDGE; Granite Mountain ("most Great Basin resources are blind, with no surface manifestation").
  • H5 residual analytic-signal window anomaly. Analytic-signal amplitude and its windowed residual on the magnetic family — short-wavelength lineaments that survive the regional field. Cheapest to build and the smallest expected gain.

When H1 would be worth revisiting

The negative result is about this construction on this proxy, not about the physics. It would be revisited with relocated hypocentres and focal mechanisms (USGS/ANSS ComCat — free and public, but unreachable from this sandbox, so a mechanism-aware proximity field has not been tested), with an independent non-catalogue fault map to score against, or with a deep model that can jointly use the release field and the structural stack instead of hand-built products.
