Why this is not "we tried a few spacings and this one looked best"

  1. Sweep. 24 spatially blocked holdouts (4 × 6 tiles, ≈ 93 × 55 km each). For every block the catalogue pixels inside the block are removed from training and the model must recover exactly those pixels from the 25 physical features; the exact official metric is then evaluated on the block with a halo, so blocking cannot change the metric's own 300 m reach.
  2. Population matching. The scored truth is the new-fault population, which is far sparser than the catalogue. Every arm is therefore scored against a density-matched subsample of the held-out catalogue (declared ratio 0.1296) as the primary population, with four other ratios swept as a sensitivity screen.
  3. Split conformal selection. Blocks are split into a selection half and a calibration half by tile parity. The calibration half yields a finite-sample one-sided bound L(s) = mean_cal(s) − q(s) with q the ceil((n+1)(1−α))-th smallest residual (Lei, G'Sell, Rinaldo, Tibshirani & Wasserman, JASA 113(523):1094–1111, 2018). α is set to the smallest value the data can support, 1/(n_cal+1) — asking for more confidence would make the bound infinite.
  4. Density admissibility (rule R3). The metric collapses in the sparse regime to DTI = T / (0.2·N + 0.8·|G|), so a submission with too many dots cannot reach the target even with perfect placement. An arm ships only if its measured full-grid dot count leaves room for 0.3345 at the declared |G|.
  5. Choice (rule R4). Among admissible arms, the largest certified floor wins. That is r = 8 px. The rules were fixed before the numbers were read, and the eligibility and split rules depend only on the truth mask and the tile index — never on an arm's score.

Why the previous best (0.2778) scored what it did — and what would beat it

The published index is DTI = TPw / (TPw + 0.2·FPw + 0.8·FNw) with a 300 m triangular kernel. In the sparse regime it collapses to a budget, DTI = T / (0.2·N + 0.8·|G|): adding mass pays only if its kernel credit exceeds 0.2 × (current score). The 0.2778 submission is the family's own 0.2708 field with every dot within 200 m of the catalogue deleted — 37,654 dots left — and the whole family sequence (0.1922 @ 121k → 0.2477 @ 60k → 0.2600 @ 44k → 0.2708 @ 40k → 0.2778 @ 38k dots) is monotone in mass removed, not in model quality. Limitations, stated up front

  • The certified floor 0.0283 is a statement about the catalogue-proxy holdout, not a promise about the leaderboard: the scored truth is the new-fault population, which no public data labels. What transfers exactly is the density side of the metric, which is where the algebra is used.
  • No DrivenData account exists in the build environment, so the rasters are the project's own hash-verified copies and the upload itself must be done by the project owner.
  • Band 10 ("distance to earthquake") has an ambiguous unit in the shipped raster; no unit was assumed anywhere (the hypothesis test uses rank and median-relative transforms only).
  • The model is a histogram gradient-boosted classifier on hand-built multi-scale features, not a deep network: no GPU and 4 GB of RAM in the build environment. The deep-model direction is ranked in Geological hypotheses.
