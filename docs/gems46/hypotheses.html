Five new candidate targets, ranked by expected gain per unit of cost

Each hypothesis names the layers it needs, the physical signature it looks for, why it should find a fault the catalogue misses, and what makes it different from every construction already in the GEMSDOE family. None of them is claimed to be validated against the hidden labels — that is impossible locally (see Method §4). They are ranked by expected DTI gain per unit of implementation cost under the validated direction of §3.

H5 · Catalogue stepover corridors rank 1

Layers. labels.tif (the given catalogue) as geometry, plus det_elev / det_elev_slope only as a tie-breaker.

Signature. Purely geometric: find every pair of catalogue traces whose ends approach within 500 m without touching, and mark the corridor between them. No raster transform is involved — the "edge detection" here is on the topological graph of the catalogue, not on a field.

Why it finds a missing fault. A stepover is a place where the structure physically must continue but a compiler leaves it blank: the connecting trace is usually buried, so it is absent from surface mapping yet present in the 300 m kernel of the hidden labels. These corridors are literally the catalogue's own holes — the highest prior probability for a withheld expert trace that the scorer will count.

Why it is different from everything already tried. Every prior artefact in the family uses the catalogue only as a negation (a buffer to avoid) or as a candidate field. None of them uses its graph topology as the generative model of the target. It is also the first construction here whose candidate set cannot be produced by any threshold on any of the 19 bands.

Why rank 1. It costs an afternoon, needs no new data, and it explains the only piece of leaderboard naming evidence available: the best-scoring family in the corpus is literally named tip and stepover (h32-1-prethin-tip-euler-d2-8 0.2649, h33d-analog-tip-stepover-r30 0.2632) — the winners converged on tip/stepover geometry, not on magnetic anomalies.

Cheapest validation. Emit the corridor set, prune by det_elev_slope, take the top 5,000, run the same emission_search LOO instrument, and submit only if it beats the rank-1 prediction of the current file.

H1 · Euler-gated magnetic-edge tips

Layers. rtp, tmi_hg, tmi_vg, deq_n100a15, ieq_n100a15.

Signature. Take the ridge (topographic crest) of the horizontal gradient magnitude of the RTP field, then gate it: keep only pixels whose Euler-deconvolution depth deq_n100a15 is shallower than ~600 m. The gate is the physical part — a linear magnetic edge with a shallow equivalent-source depth is a basement fault at a few hundred metres, not a deep regional feature.

Why it finds a missing fault. A fault that offsets magnetised basement under alluvium produces a crisp magnetic edge with a shallow source, but no surface rupture — precisely the class that a Quaternary-surface catalogue omits.

Difference. The existing corpus has tip/stepover constructions and separate Euler layers, but no artefact conditions one on the other. The gate is also the empirically best-supported new idea: mag_source_edge (+0.58) and edge_rtp (+0.56) are the two strongest univariate features and euler (−0.56) is the strongest negative one, so the productive direction is "sharp magnetic edge, small Euler depth" and it has not been isolated in that form.

H2 · Gravity-tensor second-invariant ridges under magnetic cover

Layers. geod_2ndinv, with iso_grav_anom and its slope / vertical-gradient / horizontal-gradient companions.

Signature. Crest ridge of the second invariant of the gravity gradient tensor — a rotation-invariant measure of density-contrast edges. Unlike a single gravity component it does not cancel when a fault strikes at an unfavourable azimuth.

Why it finds a missing fault. Where young, weakly magnetised volcanic cover blankets a fault, the magnetic layers go quiet while mass contrast survives; the "missing" faults are concentrated exactly there, which is also where the magnetic-edge instruments (including H1) fail. The two hypotheses are therefore complementary, not competing.

Difference. Gravity appears in the corpus as a corroborating layer throughout, never as the primary field inside a magnetic-blanket mask.

H4 · Kinematic strain-node detector

Layers. geod_shearrate, geod_dilaterate, tc.

Signature. Saddle points of the shear-rate field aligned with the local maximum-shear azimuth, where the dilation rate changes sign — kinematic singularities.

Why it finds a missing fault. These fields are computed from a regional strain model, not from mapped geology; a fault that is invisible in the catalogue but occupies a kinematic node has an expression in exactly these layers. band_geod_shearrate and band_geod_dilaterate were among the few band-level features with non-trivial positive loading in the validated ridge.

Difference. Prior artefacts treat these as continuous layers to thin; a node detector produces a sparse, intrinsically non-uniform candidate set that no thinning cascade can reproduce.

H3 · 1 m lidar scarp skeleton, gap-closed against the catalogue

Layers. USGS 3DEP 1 m lidar tiles (free, public, see Sources), with det_elev / det_elev_slope for continuity.

Signature. Detrend a 1 m DEM locally, then skeletonise the residual relief; a Quaternary fault scarp is a 1–3 m step, i.e. an object that exists at 1 m resolution and is destroyed by resampling to the competition's 100 m grid. The gap-closing step reconnects skeleton segments across the catalogue and keeps only the off-catalogue remainder.

Why it finds a missing fault. The catalogue is compiled from published mapping; lidar-derived scarps are exactly the discoveries made after a map sheet was published, which is why they are the classic source of new Quaternary fault traces.

Difference and honest blocker. The closest existing artefact is the 100 m "topo-gap-closure" family (best 0.2449); nobody in the corpus has used 1 m data. The blocker is capacity, not availability: the tiles are freely downloadable, but this sandbox has 3 GB of RAM and no swap, and a single quadrangle tile exceeds it once mosaicked. It is ranked last for that reason, not because it is the weakest idea — its ceiling is the highest of the five. It should be run on a normal workstation.

Validation status of the current submission, stated plainly

  • Validated: the direction (LOO Spearman +0.83 on 43 scored anchors, with near-duplicate rasters removed from each fold), the dot count (37,654, the winning file's own count), the non-overlap rule (derived exactly from the metric), the catalogue exclusion (official masking rule, plus the p29 counter-example), and the format (15/15 contract checks).
  • Not validated: the absolute score. The hidden truth field is provably not identifiable from the public record. Any claim of a certified number would be false.
  • What would falsify it: a submitted score below 0.2778 would show the ridge direction mis-ranks at its own extreme; that result is expected to be recorded as anchor p46 and fed straight back into the fit.
