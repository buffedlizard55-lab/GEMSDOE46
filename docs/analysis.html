Analysis and evidence

Everything on this page is either read from an organizer page, computed from the hash-pinned official rasters, or arithmetic on the metric's own definitions. Numbers that are not one of those three things are labelled as assumptions.

1. The metric, exactly

k(d) = max(1 − d/300 m, 0)
TPw = Σg∈G maxx: d(x,g)≤300 m p(x)·k(d(x,g))
FPw = Σx: p(x)>0 p(x)·[1 − maxg∈G k(d(x,g))]
FNw = Σg∈G [1 − maxx: d(x,g)≤300 m p(x)·k(d(x,g))]
DTI = TPw / (TPw + 0.2·FPw + 0.8·FNw + ε)

Read verbatim from the problem description. src/gems46/metric.py transcribes it, and tests/ checks the vectorised implementation against literal O(N²) loops and against the published worked example (TPw = 3.00, FPw = 1.89, FNw = 2.00 → 0.60).

2. Two identities that decide the competition

For binary truth: FNw = |G| − TPw. In the sparse regime (no dot is the best cover of two truth pixels): FPw = N − TPw with N the number of unit-mass cells. Substituting, with T = TPw:

DTI = T / (0.2·N + 0.8·|G|)   (★)

Cross-check. The sibling family's 0.2600 row reported 44,090 dots and 0.0893 credit per pixel, i.e. T = 3,937.2: 3937.2 / (0.2·44090 + 0.8·7905) = 0.2600 — the published score falls out of the identity exactly, from two independently reported numbers. That is the evidence that this is the algebra the leaderboard runs.

Because T ≤ |G|:

DTI ≤ |G| / (0.2·N + 0.8·|G|)   (★★)

What this rules out, immediately

  • More than 86,541 dots cannot reach 0.3345, however good the field is.
  • Scale is a no-op for graded maps. DTI(λ) = λT/(0.2λN + 0.8|G|) is strictly increasing in λ, so a probabilistic map is dominated by its own thresholded support: the artefact must be 0/1.
  • Deleting useless mass is a score move, not a cosmetic one. The family's own sequence — 0.1922 @ 121,131 → 0.2477 @ 60,069 → 0.2600 @ 44,090 → 0.2708 @ 40,199 → 0.2778 @ 37,654 — is monotone in mass removed, and 0.2778 is exactly the 0.2708 field with every dot within 2 px of the catalogue deleted.

3. The 24-block sweep (this repository's own experiment)

4 × 6 tiles, ≈ 93 × 55 km each. For each block: train with every catalogue pixel inside the block removed, then recover exactly those pixels from the 25 physical features; score with the exact metric plus a halo. 8 blocks lie outside the surveyed area and are excluded by rule R1 (they contain no held-out truth; every arm scores 0 there). Blocks are split by tile parity into a selection half and a calibration half (rule R2), and the calibration half supplies the split-conformal quantile at the smallest alpha the data supports, α = 1/(n_cal+1) = 0.1000.

The primary truth population is pop_thin: a deterministic 12.96 % subsample of the held-out catalogue, i.e. density-matched to the scored new-fault population. The conformal quantile is the ceil((n_cal+1)(1−α))-th smallest residual sj(s) − meancal(s), and L = meancal − q is the finite-sample one-sided lower bound; a negative L means the calibration half cannot certify the arm at this confidence at all. The two dot columns are the sweep's own per-block extrapolation (median per block × 24) and the measured full-grid count — the first overestimates the second for two measured reasons (cf. docs/IRREGULARITIES.md IR-46-06).

Rules, in the order they are applied

  1. R1 eligibility — a block enters iff it holds ≥ 1 held-out truth pixel (truth-mask rule, score-independent).
  2. R2 split — selection / calibration = tile parity (i+j)%2, so both halves have comparable fault density and comparable distance to the survey edge.
  3. R3 admissibility — an arm ships only if its dot count leaves room for 0.3345 under (★★) at the declared |G|: N ≤ 86,541. Applied to the measured count in the shipped run.
  4. R4 choice — among admissible arms, the largest certified floor. That is r = 8 px.

4. What is inside the shipped file

  • 39,108 positive cells, 0.757 % of the 5,167,373-px surveyed footprint, one dot per ≥ 800 m cell after greedy non-maximum suppression on expected metric credit.
  • Ordering is by expected marginal credit ΔT(x) = Σδ π(x+δ)k(|δ|), not by probability: ordering by π alone emits several parallel copies of a three-pixel-wide ridge and pays the 0.2-per-dot tax on each. The emitter stops when ΔT(x) < 0.2 × 0.26 — a pre-declared bar, not a tuned one.
  • Model: histogram gradient boosting on 25 hand-built multi-scale features (L2 ridge curvature at three scales on detrended elevation, official tilt-curvature edge band, TMI, RTP, isostatic gravity, gradient/edge and cover proxies). No distance-to-known-fault feature exists in the stack.
  • Prior correction: the training sample is stratified (all positives, 3× negatives), so log-odds are re-centred to the population prevalence (train 0.2500 → population 0.01180, shift 3.3290) before the probability field is used by the emitter.
  • Independent re-read audit of the written bytes: single band, float32, 3730 × 3292, EPSG:32611, template transform, unique positive value 1.0, range [0, 1], positives = 39,108 — checks JSON.
  • DTI on the catalogue proxy: 0.0920. This number is reported because it is the only score computable without the private labels, and it is not the certified floor and not a leaderboard prediction: the catalogue is the population the scored task excludes.

5. Audit trail

Two defects were found by re-running the pipeline end to end and are documented with their measurements in IRREGULARITIES.md:

  • IR-46-05 — the emitter's candidate-list guard silently truncated emissions (5,596 dots instead of 39,108 at r = 8 px on the full grid). The 24-block sweep was verified bit-identical before and after the fix (per-block DTI and dot counts compared line by line), so the selection evidence is unaffected; the previously produced full-grid file was superseded.
  • IR-46-10 — the conformal driver was silently using the contiguous split (a positional argument landed in the scheme parameter), and the file auditor contained a vacuous array comparison. Both are fixed; selection now runs through one implementation (src/gems46/conformal.py) used by both entry points.
