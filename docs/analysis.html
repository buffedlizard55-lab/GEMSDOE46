How the metric behaves

A sparse edit can help—sometimes.

The official task uses a distance-weighted Tversky score with a triangular 300 m support, `alpha=0.2`, and `beta=0.8`. In simplified notation consistent with the published definition, each labeled fault pixel receives its maximum prediction credit from nearby pixels:

credit(g) = maxₓ p(x) · max(0, 1 − distance(x,g) / 300 m)

True-positive credit sums those values; the false-negative term sums the remaining credit; predicted mass receives a distance-weighted false-positive cost. The published coefficients weight false negatives more heavily than false positives, but that does not reveal why any named entry scored as it did.

Because per-truth credit uses a maximum, redundant probability mass near an already-covered label may add little TP credit. Removing probability far from evaluation labels may reduce false-positive cost. Conversely, deleting a prediction within 300 m of a withheld fault can remove useful credit. An existing fault catalogue is not the hidden answer, and catalogue exclusion is not itself a reward.

What we can infer

Metric mechanism ≠ causal attribution.

If H33 were an over-dense map and a pruning edit removed low-value mass, a higher DTI would be plausible. But we do not know that H33 received 0.2778, which artifact was uploaded, or why `extradr19` earned 0.2778.

No causal explanation is justified without the organizer receipt and exact submitted raster.
